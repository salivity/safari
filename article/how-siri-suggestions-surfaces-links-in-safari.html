How Siri Suggestions Surfaces Links in Safari

This article explains the mechanisms behind the Siri Suggestions section on the Safari Start Page, detailing how Apple’s on-device machine learning scans the Mail and Calendar applications to automatically extract, rank, and display relevant web links based on your current context, schedule, and communication habits.

On-Device Indexing

Siri Suggestions relies on a system-level framework that continuously indexes data locally on your iPhone, iPad, or Mac. Using the Apple Neural Engine, Siri scans the contents of Apple's native applications without sending your personal data to external servers. When a URL is detected inside the Mail or Calendar app, the system logs the link alongside metadata such as the sender, the time the link was received, and the text surrounding the URL.

Extracting Links from Mail

Siri does not display every link found in your inbox. Instead, it filters out newsletters, automated receipts, and spam to focus on high-priority communications. Links from Mail are surfaced on the Safari Start Page based on the following criteria:

  • Sender Relevance: Links sent by people in your Contacts, VIP list, or frequent email threads are prioritized.
  • Recency: URLs from emails received or opened within the last few days are ranked higher than older messages.
  • Actionable Language: Siri uses natural language processing to detect phrases near the link, such as "check this out," "review this document," or "please read," which indicates the link requires your attention.

Surfacing Links from Calendar

Links extracted from the Calendar app are heavily dependent on time and scheduling context. Siri monitors your upcoming events and looks for URLs placed in the location field, the dedicated URL field, or the event notes.

  • Time Sensitivity: If you have a calendar event starting soon, Siri pushes the associated link to the Safari Start Page just before the event begins. This is commonly used for surfacing video conferencing links (like Zoom, Webex, or Google Meet) or collaborative documents linked in a meeting invite.
  • Expiration: Once the calendar event has passed, Siri automatically lowers the priority of the link or removes it from the Start Page entirely to keep the section relevant.

Contextual Ranking

Because space in the Siri Suggestions section is limited, Safari must decide which links from Mail and Calendar are most relevant at the exact moment you open a new tab. Siri calculates a relevance score using your current context:

  • Time of Day: Work-related links from Mail and Calendar are prioritized during your typical working hours, while personal links may be surfaced in the evening.
  • Device Usage: If you open a specific email, read it, and then immediately open Safari, Siri assumes you are looking for the information discussed in that email and will place its links at the top of the Suggestions list.

Privacy and Customization

All processing for Siri Suggestions happens locally on your device. Safari does not share the contents of your emails or calendar events with Apple or third-party websites until you actually click on a suggested link.

If you want to stop Safari from surfacing links from these apps, you can disable this feature in your device's System Settings (or Settings on iOS). Navigate to the Siri & Search menu, select either Mail or Calendar, and toggle off the option to "Show in App" or "Suggest App." You can also customize the Safari Start Page directly by clicking the settings icon in the bottom right corner of the browser window and toggling off Siri Suggestions completely.
