How Live Captions Transcribe Safari Audio on macOS and iOS

This article explains the underlying technology that enables Live Captions on macOS and iOS to transcribe audio from the Safari web browser in real time. By combining system-level audio interception through Core Audio with the dedicated processing power of the Apple Neural Engine, Apple devices convert spoken dialogue from web videos, podcasts, and audio streams into readable text directly on the device. This seamless process happens locally in milliseconds, ensuring minimal latency, consistent accuracy, and complete user privacy.

System-Level Audio Interception

Live Captions do not rely on Safari extensions, website code, or web-based APIs to function. Instead, the feature operates at the operating system level using Apple's Core Audio framework.

When you play a video or audio file in Safari, the browser decodes the media and sends the digital audio stream to the system's central audio server. Before this audio reaches your device's speakers or headphones, the operating system duplicates the audio buffer. It sends one copy to your audio output and routes the other directly to the system's accessibility speech-recognition service. Because it taps into the audio pipeline at the OS level, Live Captions can transcribe any sound coming from Safari, regardless of the website or media player being used.

Audio Pre-Processing

Once the system captures the raw audio stream from Safari, it must prepare the data for transcription. The audio undergoes instant pre-processing where it is downsampled and filtered to isolate human speech frequencies from background music or web-based sound effects.

The system then slices this continuous audio stream into tiny, overlapping acoustic frames, each lasting just a few milliseconds. This converts the continuous wave of sound from Safari into discrete chunks of digital data that a machine learning model can read.

Hardware Acceleration via the Neural Engine

Real-time speech-to-text conversion requires immense computational power. To prevent Safari and the rest of the operating system from lagging, macOS and iOS offload the transcription workload to the Apple Neural Engine (ANE).

The ANE is a specialized hardware processor built into Apple Silicon (A-series chips on iOS and M-series chips on macOS). It is designed specifically to execute matrix multiplication and machine learning algorithms at high speeds while consuming minimal battery power. This hardware requirement is why Live Captions are strictly limited to newer Apple devices equipped with Apple Silicon.

Acoustic and Language Modeling

Inside the Neural Engine, the pre-processed audio frames pass through two primary machine learning models working in tandem:

  1. The Acoustic Model: This neural network analyzes the audio frames and predicts the most likely phonemes—the distinct units of sound that make up spoken language.
  2. The Language Model: This model takes the stream of phonemes and assembles them into coherent words and sentences. It uses statistical probabilities and contextual awareness to differentiate between homophones (such as "two" and "too") and dynamically inserts punctuation as the speaker continues talking.

Dynamic Rendering and Local Privacy

As the machine learning models generate text, the operating system pushes the output to the floating Live Captions window overlaid on your screen. Because human speech relies heavily on context, you will often see the Live Captions interface display a word and then instantly change it a split second later. This happens because the language model is continuously analyzing the ongoing Safari audio stream and retroactively correcting previous words based on new context.

Because this entire pipeline—from Core Audio interception to Neural Engine processing—executes locally on your device's hardware, the transcription happens with near-zero latency. Furthermore, no audio data from your Safari browsing session is ever sent to Apple's servers or the cloud, allowing the feature to work entirely offline while keeping your web activity private.
