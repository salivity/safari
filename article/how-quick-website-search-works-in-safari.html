How Quick Website Search Works in Safari

This article provides a complete overview of the Quick Website Search feature in Apple's Safari web browser. It explains the underlying mechanism of how Safari automatically detects and remembers a website's internal search engine, how you can use this feature to search sites like Wikipedia or YouTube directly from the address bar, and how to manage your saved websites on both Mac and iOS devices.

How Safari Learns a Website's Search Engine

Safari does not come pre-loaded with a massive database of every website's search function. Instead, Quick Website Search works by learning from your browsing habits through two main methods:

  1. URL Structure Analysis: When you visit a website like YouTube or Wikipedia and use its built-in search bar, Safari observes the web address (URL) of the results page. It identifies where your search terms appear in that URL and saves the structural formula.
  2. OpenSearch Detection: Many modern websites use a technology standard called OpenSearch. This standard includes a hidden piece of code that explicitly tells web browsers how to format a search URL for that specific site. Safari reads this code the moment you visit the webpage.

Once Safari captures this information, it pairs the search formula with the website's name and adds the site to your personal Quick Website Search list.

Searching Directly from the Address Bar

After Safari has learned a website's search function, you no longer need to navigate to the site first to search within it. You can bypass your default search engine (such as Google or Bing) entirely by using Safari's Smart Search Field (the address bar).

To perform a direct search:

  1. Click or tap the Safari address bar.
  2. Type the name of the website, followed by a space, and then your search query (for example, type wikipedia Apollo 11 or youtube how to bake bread).
  3. Look at the drop-down menu that appears below the address bar.
  4. Click or tap the option that says Search [Website] for "[Your Query]".

Safari will take your search terms, insert them into the saved URL formula for that specific website, and take you straight to the site's internal search results page.

Managing Quick Website Search

If you want to view which websites Safari has saved, remove specific sites, or turn the feature off entirely, you can do so in your device's settings.

On a Mac:

  1. Open Safari and click Safari in the top menu bar.
  2. Select Settings (or Preferences on older macOS versions).
  3. Go to the Search tab.
  4. Check or uncheck the box for Enable Quick Website Search.
  5. Click Manage Websites to see a list of all saved sites. From here, you can select individual websites and click Remove to delete them.

On an iPhone or iPad:

  1. Open the Settings app.
  2. Scroll down and tap Safari.
  3. Tap Quick Website Search.
  4. Use the toggle at the top to turn the feature on or off.
  5. To delete a specific saved website, swipe left on its name in the list and tap Delete, or tap Edit in the corner to remove multiple sites at once.
