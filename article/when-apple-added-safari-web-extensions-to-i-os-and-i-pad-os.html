When Apple Added Safari WebExtensions to iOS and iPadOS

This article provides a direct overview of when Apple introduced third-party WebExtensions to the Safari web browser on iOS and iPadOS. It covers the initial developer announcement, the official public release date, and how this update transformed the mobile browsing experience by bringing desktop-class extensions to the iPhone and iPad.

Apple officially released support for third-party Safari WebExtensions on iOS and iPadOS on September 20, 2021. This feature rolled out to the general public as a core component of the iOS 15 and iPadOS 15 operating system updates.

Before the public release, Apple first announced the arrival of mobile WebExtensions on June 7, 2021, during its annual Worldwide Developers Conference (WWDC). This June announcement gave developers a three-month beta testing period to build new extensions or port their existing desktop extensions to Apple's mobile platforms before the September launch.

The introduction of WebExtensions on iOS 15 and iPadOS 15 was a continuation of an effort Apple began the previous year. In 2020, Apple introduced the WebExtensions API to the Mac with the release of Safari 14. The WebExtensions API is a cross-browser system used by other major browsers like Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, and Microsoft Edge. By bringing this same standard to iOS and iPadOS in 2021, Apple made it simple for developers to bring extensions from other browsers directly to the iPhone and iPad with minimal code changes.

Prior to September 2021, iOS and iPadOS users were restricted to basic content blockers and limited "Share" menu actions inside Safari. The iOS 15 and iPadOS 15 update allowed users to download full-featured extensions directly from the App Store. This enabled software such as password managers, advanced ad blockers, price trackers, and custom web-page styling tools to run natively inside mobile Safari for the first time.
