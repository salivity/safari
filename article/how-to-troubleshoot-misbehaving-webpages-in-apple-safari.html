How to Troubleshoot Misbehaving Webpages in Apple Safari

When a webpage fails to load properly, displays broken layouts, or refuses to respond in Safari, the culprit is usually corrupted site data or a conflicting third-party browser extension. This article provides a straightforward guide on how to quickly isolate and fix these issues by testing the problematic webpage in a clean Private Browsing window and temporarily disabling your installed Safari extensions.

Method 1: Test in a Clean Private Browsing Window

A Private Browsing window acts as a clean slate. It ignores your stored cookies, cached data, and browsing history. In recent versions of Safari, it also disables all extensions by default. Testing a webpage here tells you immediately if your local browser data is causing the problem.

On a Mac:

  1. Open Safari.
  2. Click File in the top menu bar and select New Private Window, or press Shift + Command + N on your keyboard.
  3. Navigate to the misbehaving webpage.

On an iPhone or iPad:

  1. Open Safari.
  2. Tap the Tabs button (two overlapping squares) in the bottom-right corner (or top-right on iPad).
  3. Tap Private in the bottom-left corner, then tap the + icon to open a new private tab.
  4. Load the problematic webpage.

How to interpret the results:

  • If the webpage works normally: The issue is caused by corrupted cache, bad cookies, or an active extension in your standard browsing session. To fix this, go to Safari Settings > Privacy > Manage Website Data, search for the specific website, and click Remove to clear its data.
  • If the webpage still fails: The issue is likely on the website's end, or it is related to your network connection.

Method 2: Temporarily Disable All Safari Extensions

Browser extensions—especially ad blockers, privacy tools, and script managers—frequently interfere with how modern webpages load. Disabling them temporarily is the best way to see if an extension is breaking the site.

On a Mac:

  1. With Safari open, click Safari in the top menu bar and select Settings (or Preferences on older macOS versions).
  2. Click the Extensions tab at the top of the window.
  3. Uncheck the box next to every listed extension to turn them off.
  4. Return to the webpage and press Command + R to reload it.

On an iPhone or iPad:

  1. Open the problematic webpage in Safari.
  2. Tap the Page Settings button (the "aA" icon or the puzzle piece icon) on the left side of the address bar.
  3. Select Manage Extensions.
  4. Toggle all extensions to the Off position and tap Done.
  5. Refresh the webpage.

How to pinpoint the problematic extension: If the webpage loads correctly with all extensions turned off, an extension is definitely the cause. Go back into your Extensions settings and turn them back on one by one, reloading the webpage after enabling each one. When the webpage breaks again, you have found the conflicting extension. You can then choose to leave it disabled for that specific site, check if the extension needs an update, or uninstall it completely.

Quick Tip: Reload Without Content Blockers

If you suspect an ad blocker is breaking a page and you do not want to disable all extensions manually, you can bypass content blockers for a single reload.

  • On a Mac: Click and hold the Reload button (the circular arrow) in the address bar, then select Reload Without Content Blockers.
  • On an iPhone or iPad: Tap and hold the Reload button in the address bar, or tap the aA icon, and select Turn Off Content Blockers.
