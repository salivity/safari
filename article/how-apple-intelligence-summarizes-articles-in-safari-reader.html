How Apple Intelligence Summarizes Articles in Safari Reader

This article explains the technical process behind Apple Intelligence's ability to automatically generate concise summaries and tables of contents in Safari's Reader View. It covers how the browser isolates webpage text, utilizes on-device large language models to analyze the information, and structures the output for quick user comprehension while maintaining user privacy.

1. Isolating the Core Content

Before Apple Intelligence processes any information, Safari's traditional Reader View technology must clean up the webpage. When you open an article in Reader View, Safari analyzes the webpage's Document Object Model (DOM). It strips away clutter such as advertisements, sidebars, navigation menus, and comment sections. This leaves behind only the raw body text, relevant images, and structural HTML headings (like H1, H2, and H3 tags). This clean text serves as the prompt for the AI.

2. Engaging the On-Device Foundation Model

Once the text is isolated, it is passed to Apple Intelligence. Rather than sending the webpage data to an external server, the processing happens locally on the device using Apple's ~3-billion-parameter on-device language model. This process relies heavily on the device's Neural Engine (available on the A17 Pro and M-series chips) to read and "understand" the text rapidly without draining the battery.

3. Applying Specialized Adapters

Apple’s core on-device model is a generalist, meaning it knows a little bit about everything. To perform the specific task of summarizing an article, the system dynamically loads a "LoRA" (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapter. This is a small, specialized module fine-tuned specifically for reading comprehension and summarization. The adapter instructs the model on how to weigh the importance of different sentences, ensuring it captures the core thesis of the article rather than getting distracted by minor details.

4. Generating the Concise Summary

With the summarization adapter active, the model performs "abstractive summarization." Instead of just copying and pasting the first few sentences of the article, the AI synthesizes the entire text and writes a brand-new, brief paragraph. It identifies the main subject, the primary conclusion, and the most critical supporting facts, condensing minutes of reading into a few seconds of scanning.

5. Constructing the Table of Contents

Simultaneously, Apple Intelligence generates the interactive Table of Contents. The system does this using two methods:

  • HTML Tag Analysis: If the original writer used proper subheadings (H2, H3) in the article, the AI reads those headings, shortens them if they are too long, and uses them as the structural backbone.
  • Semantic Grouping: If the article is just a long wall of text with no subheadings, the AI analyzes the text for topic shifts. When it detects that the article has moved on to a new point, it groups those paragraphs together and generates a short, original title for that section.

Safari then links these generated titles to the corresponding paragraphs in the Reader View. Tapping an item in the Table of Contents smoothly scrolls the page directly to that specific section of the text.

6. Safety and Fact-Checking Guardrails

To prevent "hallucinations" (where the AI invents false information), the model is strictly constrained to the text provided by the webpage. It is programmed not to pull in outside knowledge to fill in blanks. If an article is too short, lacks substantive text, or is formatted in a way the AI cannot confidently parse, Safari simply disables the summary and Table of Contents options for that specific page to avoid generating inaccurate results.
