Does Safari Distraction Control Sync Across Your Devices?

This article explains how Apple's Distraction Control feature in the Safari web browser manages the webpage elements you choose to hide. It clarifies that hidden items are stored locally on each individual device rather than syncing across your Apple devices via iCloud, and details why changes to a website's underlying layout or code will cause those hidden elements to automatically reappear.

Distraction Control does not sync hidden items across your devices. When you use the feature to vanish an overlay, newsletter sign-up banner, or floating video player on your iPhone, that exact same element will still be visible if you visit the webpage on your iPad or Mac. Safari saves your preferences locally on the specific device where you performed the action.

The primary reason for this local-only storage is the difference in how websites are built for different screen sizes. A mobile webpage viewed on an iPhone often uses entirely different code, layouts, and element names compared to the desktop version of the same site viewed on a Mac. Because the underlying structure of the page varies from device to device, syncing the removal of a specific visual element across platforms would not work reliably.

Furthermore, the elements you hide are not permanently blocked if the website updates. Distraction Control is designed to hide static page elements, and it works by remembering the specific location and code structure of the item you selected. Hidden elements will remain invisible during future visits to that website only as long as the webpage's layout remains exactly the same.

If a website developer updates the page layout, alters the underlying HTML code, or changes the content within the space you hid, Safari will no longer recognize the element as the one you wanted to remove. Consequently, the hidden item will reset and reappear on your screen. This is also why Distraction Control does not function as an ad blocker; because advertisements dynamically refresh and change their code frequently, hiding an ad space will only remove it until the page reloads or serves a new ad.

If a webpage layout has not changed, your hidden items will stay hidden on that specific device until you clear your Safari browsing history and website data, or until you manually restore them by tapping the Page Menu icon in the address bar and selecting the option to show hidden items.
