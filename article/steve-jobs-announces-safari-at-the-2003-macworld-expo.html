Steve Jobs Announces Safari at the 2003 Macworld Expo

Steve Jobs first announced the Safari web browser on January 7, 2003, during his keynote address at the Macworld Conference & Expo in San Francisco. This article covers the details of that historic announcement, the reasons Apple developed its own browser to replace Microsoft's Internet Explorer, and the key features that defined Safari's initial release on Mac OS X.

The announcement marked a major shift in Apple’s software strategy. Since a 1997 agreement with Microsoft, Internet Explorer had served as the default web browser on all Macintosh computers. With that five-year agreement coming to an end and development on Internet Explorer for Mac slowing down, Apple secretly developed its own browser to ensure the platform had a fast, modern way to access the web.

During the January 7 keynote, Jobs introduced Safari as the "fastest browser on the Mac" and released it as a free public beta that same day for Mac OS X v10.2 (Jaguar). To build the browser, Apple’s engineering team, led by Don Melton, utilized the open-source KHTML rendering engine from the KDE project. Apple adapted this code into what became known as WebKit, optimizing it specifically for Mac hardware and software.

Jobs focused on three main pillars during the presentation: speed, innovation, and freedom from clutter. In live demonstrations, he showed that Safari loaded HTML pages more than three times faster than Internet Explorer for Mac and ran JavaScript over twice as fast.

Alongside raw performance, Jobs highlighted several innovative features that were not standard in browsers at the time:

  • Integrated Google Search: Safari was one of the first major browsers to include a dedicated search bar directly in the main toolbar, allowing users to search Google without navigating to the homepage first.
  • SnapBack: This feature allowed users to instantly jump back to their original search results or the top-level page of a website with a single click, regardless of how many links they had clicked through.
  • Streamlined Bookmarks: Instead of complex drop-down menus, Safari managed bookmarks using the same intuitive interface design found in Apple's iTunes software.
  • Pop-Up Blocking: Built-in tools allowed users to easily block intrusive pop-up advertisements.

Following the January 2003 Macworld announcement and subsequent months of beta testing, Apple released the official Safari 1.0 version on June 23, 2003. Later that year, with the October release of Mac OS X v10.3 (Panther), Safari officially replaced Internet Explorer as the default browser on all Mac computers.
