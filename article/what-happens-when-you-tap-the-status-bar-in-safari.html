What Happens When You Tap the Status Bar in Safari

This article provides a quick overview of the built-in "scroll to top" function on iPhones and iPads. It explains exactly what happens when you tap the very top edge of the screen or status bar while scrolling down a long webpage in the Safari browser, how this gesture affects the browser's interface, and where specifically to tap depending on your device model.

Instant Scroll to the Top

When you are scrolling down a lengthy webpage in Safari and tap the very top edge of your screen (the status bar), the browser immediately and rapidly scrolls all the way back to the very top of the webpage.

Instead of swiping down repeatedly with your thumb to return to the beginning of an article or website, this single tap acts as an instant elevator back to the header of the page.

Restoring Hidden Toolbars

Tapping the top edge of the screen also restores the Safari user interface. When you scroll down a webpage, Safari automatically minimizes the address bar and hides the bottom navigation toolbar to maximize your reading space.

When you trigger the scroll-to-top gesture by tapping the status bar, Safari simultaneously expands the minimized address bar and brings back all hidden navigation buttons (Back, Forward, Share, Bookmarks, and Tabs) once the page reaches the top.

Where to Tap on Different Devices

To trigger this action without accidentally clicking a link on the webpage, you must tap the system status bar—the area that displays the time, Wi-Fi signal, and battery icon.

  • iPhones with Face ID (Notch or Dynamic Island): Tap the top-left corner (on the time) or the top-right corner (on the battery icon). You can also tap the extreme top edge of the glass directly above the camera cutout.
  • iPhones with a Home Button: Tap anywhere along the narrow status bar at the very top edge of the screen.
  • iPads: Tap the status bar at the very top edge of the display, either in the center or on the corners where the system icons are located.

Top vs. Bottom Address Bar Behavior

On iPhones running iOS 15 or later, the behavior slightly depends on where you have positioned your Safari address bar:

  • Bottom Address Bar (Default): Because the address bar is at the bottom of the screen, tapping anywhere along the top edge of the display will immediately scroll the webpage to the top.
  • Top Address Bar (Single Tab): As you scroll down, the address bar shrinks into a tiny text bar at the top of the screen. If you tap the website name in that shrunken bar, it will only expand the address bar to let you type a new URL. To scroll to the top of the webpage, you must tap above the website name, directly on the clock or battery icon.
