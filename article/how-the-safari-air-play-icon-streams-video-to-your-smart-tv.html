How the Safari AirPlay Icon Streams Video to Your Smart TV

This article explains the underlying technology of the AirPlay icon found inside Safari web video players and how it seamlessly streams media to an Apple TV or AirPlay-compatible smart TV. By utilizing local network discovery, Safari's built-in web engine, and direct media URL handoffs, the browser allows your television to fetch and play web videos independently while turning your computer or mobile device into a remote control.

Automatic Device Discovery via Bonjour

Before you even click play on a video, your Apple device is quietly scanning your local Wi-Fi network using an Apple technology called Bonjour. Bonjour is a zero-configuration networking protocol that allows devices on the same network to automatically discover each other without needing manual IP address entries. When your iPhone, iPad, or Mac detects an Apple TV or an AirPlay 2-compatible smart TV on the same Wi-Fi network, it signals to the operating system that an external playback target is available.

WebKit and HTML5 Integration

The Safari browser is built on an engine called WebKit. When you load a webpage containing a modern HTML5 video player, WebKit automatically scans the code. Because the operating system already knows an AirPlay receiver is nearby (thanks to Bonjour), WebKit dynamically injects the AirPlay icon directly into the video player's control bar. Web developers can also use specific Apple-provided APIs to customize this button so it matches the design of their custom video players.

The URL Handoff (How the Video Actually Streams)

A common misconception is that your phone or computer downloads the video and beams it to the TV. While this happens during screen mirroring, clicking the AirPlay icon inside a Safari video player works much more efficiently.

When you select your TV from the AirPlay menu, Safari extracts the direct web address (URL) of the video stream—typically formatted in Apple’s HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) protocol—and sends only that text link to the TV.

Once the TV receives the link, the TV connects directly to the internet to download and play the video itself. This method provides several advantages:

  • Zero Quality Loss: The video is not compressed a second time by your device; the TV plays the highest quality stream available directly from the source.
  • Battery Efficiency: Because your phone or laptop isn't processing the video, its battery drain is minimal. You can even lock your iPhone screen, and the video will keep playing.
  • Multitasking: You can switch to other tabs in Safari or use other apps without interrupting the video on the TV.

Local Streaming Fallback

If the video you are watching in Safari is a local file stored on your device, or if it uses a web format the TV cannot fetch on its own, AirPlay switches methods. In this scenario, Safari decodes the video on your device, compresses it into an H.264 or HEVC video stream, and sends the actual video and audio data over your local Wi-Fi network to the TV using a secure, peer-to-peer connection.

Real-Time Playback Control

Once the video is playing on the television, Safari shifts the video player on your device into a remote control interface. The browser maintains an active, encrypted network connection with the TV. When you click pause, adjust the volume, or scrub through the timeline in Safari, your device sends small data packets containing these commands over the Wi-Fi network, instructing the TV on how to manipulate the video stream in real time.
