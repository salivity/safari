What Happens If You Turn Off Check for Apple Pay in Safari

Disabling the "Check for Apple Pay" setting in Safari's privacy preferences prevents websites from automatically detecting whether you have an active Apple Pay account and a valid card in your Apple Wallet. While turning this feature off enhances your online privacy by reducing browser fingerprinting and blocking merchant tracking, it also makes online checkouts less convenient by hiding the Apple Pay button on most e-commerce websites. This article explains the exact consequences of disabling this setting, how it alters your online shopping experience, and the specific privacy benefits it provides.

How the Setting Works

By default, the "Check for Apple Pay" setting is turned on in Safari for iOS, iPadOS, and macOS. When you visit an online store, the website runs a script that asks Safari if your device is capable of making Apple Pay transactions and if you have a card set up in your Wallet app.

If you disable this setting, you block Safari from answering that question. Websites are entirely cut off from communicating with your Apple Wallet to verify your payment readiness.

The Impact on Online Shopping

Turning off this setting directly impacts how you pay for goods and services within the Safari browser. Here is what happens during the checkout process:

  • Hidden Apple Pay Buttons: Most websites only display the "Buy with Apple Pay" button if Safari confirms you have an active card in your Wallet. With the setting disabled, websites assume you do not use Apple Pay and will completely hide the button.
  • Defaulting to Manual Entry: Because the Apple Pay option is hidden, websites will force you to use standard checkout methods. You will have to manually type in your credit card numbers, use Safari AutoFill, or use third-party payment processors like PayPal.
  • Setup Prompts: A small number of websites show the Apple Pay button to anyone using Safari, regardless of their settings. However, if you click the button with the privacy setting disabled, the transaction may fail, or the website may redirect you to instructions on how to set up Apple Pay rather than processing your payment.

The Impact on Your Privacy

The primary reason to disable "Check for Apple Pay" is to increase your digital privacy while browsing the web. Leaving it turned off provides two main privacy benefits:

  • Reduces Browser Fingerprinting: Ad networks and data brokers use browser fingerprinting to track you across the internet without using cookies. They do this by collecting tiny pieces of technical data about your device. Knowing that you are an Apple user who actively uses Apple Pay is a valuable data point that helps trackers build a more accurate profile of you. Disabling the check removes this data point.
  • Prevents Demographic Profiling: Studies have shown that some merchants and travel sites alter prices or highlight different products based on a user's perceived wealth. Blocking a website from knowing you have an active credit card linked to an Apple device prevents them from immediately categorizing you into a specific consumer demographic the moment you load their webpage.

What Is Not Affected

Disabling "Check for Apple Pay" in Safari does not deactivate your Apple Wallet. The following features will continue to work normally:

  • In-Store Purchases: You can still use your iPhone or Apple Watch at physical retail terminals using Tap to Pay.
  • In-App Purchases: You can still use Apple Pay inside native apps downloaded from the App Store (such as the Uber, Amazon, or Target apps), as these do not rely on Safari's web browser settings.
  • Safari AutoFill: If you have credit cards saved directly in Safari's AutoFill settings (separate from the Apple Pay system), Safari will still offer to fill in your card numbers on standard checkout forms.
