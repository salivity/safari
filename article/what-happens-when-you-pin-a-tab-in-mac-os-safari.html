What Happens When You Pin a Tab in macOS Safari

Pinning a tab in the Safari web browser on macOS locks a website to the left side of the tab bar for quick and permanent access. This article covers the exact behaviors triggered when you pin a tab, including its visual transformation, how it handles internal and external links, its persistence across different browser windows and sessions, and how you can manage or remove the pinned site.

Visual Transformation and Placement

When you drag a tab to the far-left side of the tab bar—or Control-click the tab and select "Pin Tab"—the tab immediately shrinks into a compact square. Instead of displaying the full page title, the pinned tab only shows the website's icon (favicon) or a colored square with the first letter of the website's domain name.

Additionally, the standard "X" close button is removed from the tab. This prevents you from accidentally closing the webpage during normal browsing.

Persistence Across Windows and Sessions

Pinned tabs are designed to be permanent fixtures in your browser. Once a tab is pinned, the following behaviors apply:

  • New Windows: Every time you open a new standard Safari window, your pinned tabs will automatically appear on the left side of the tab bar in that new window. (Note: Pinned tabs do not appear in Private Browsing windows).
  • Quitting Safari: If you completely quit Safari or restart your Mac, your pinned tabs are saved. They will automatically reload the next time you launch the browser.
  • Tab Synchronization: If you have multiple Safari windows open at the same time, interacting with a pinned tab in one window updates it across all other open windows.

Link and Navigation Behavior

Pinned tabs handle navigation differently than standard tabs to ensure you do not lose the original webpage you pinned:

  • Same-Site Links: If you click a link that leads to another page on the exact same website (domain), that page will load directly inside the pinned tab.
  • External Links: If you click a link that leads to a completely different website, Safari will automatically open that link in a brand-new, standard tab to the right. This keeps your pinned tab anchored to its original website.

Background Activity and Audio Controls

Pinned tabs remain active in the background. This means web applications like email clients, messaging platforms, or social media feeds will continue to update and receive notifications even when you are not actively viewing them.

If a pinned tab starts playing audio or video, a small speaker icon will replace the website icon on the tab. You can click this speaker icon directly to mute or unmute the audio without having to switch to the tab itself.

Removing a Pinned Tab

Because the close button is removed from pinned tabs, you must take specific actions to remove them:

  • Unpinning: You can drag the pinned tab back toward the right side of the tab bar, and it will expand back into a normal tab. Alternatively, you can Control-click the pinned tab and select "Unpin Tab."
  • Closing: You can close a pinned tab directly by clicking on it to make it active and pressing Command + W on your keyboard, or by Control-clicking the tab and selecting "Close Tab."
