Why the Safari Reader View Button is Sometimes Grayed Out

Safari’s Reader View is a convenient feature that removes advertisements, navigation menus, and other distractions to present online articles in a clean, readable format. However, users frequently notice that the Reader View icon in the address bar is grayed out or completely unavailable. This article explains the specific technical reasons behind this limitation, including page layout requirements, HTML coding structures, loading speeds, and intentional restrictions by website owners, as well as how you can troubleshoot the issue.

The Webpage Is Not an Article

Reader View is specifically designed for text-heavy articles and blog posts. Safari uses an algorithm to scan a webpage and determine if it contains a continuous body of text. If you are on a website’s homepage, a search engine results page, a forum, or an e-commerce product grid, Safari recognizes that there is no single article to extract. As a result, the Reader View button remains disabled.

Poor Website HTML Structure

For Safari to isolate an article from the rest of a webpage, it relies on standard HTML formatting, known as semantic markup. Safari looks for specific code tags—such as <article>, <main>, and <p> (paragraph)—to identify where the story begins and ends.

If a web developer builds a site using outdated code or generic containers (like <div> tags) without proper labels, Safari cannot distinguish the main text from the sidebar or footer. When the browser cannot confidently identify the article, it grays out the Reader View button to prevent displaying a broken or incomplete page.

The Page Has Not Finished Loading

Safari does not enable the Reader View button until the webpage has fully loaded its core content. If you are on a slow internet connection, or if the website is bogged down by heavy advertisements and tracking scripts, the button will remain grayed out until the loading process is complete.

Heavy Use of JavaScript

Many modern websites use JavaScript frameworks to load content dynamically. Instead of sending a complete text document to your browser immediately, the website sends a blank template and fetches the text a moment later. Because Safari checks for Reader View compatibility very early in the loading process, it may scan the page before the text actually appears, mistakenly concluding that there is no article to read.

Intentional Blocking by Publishers

Some websites rely heavily on advertising revenue or use "soft paywalls" (which hide text behind a subscription pop-up after the page loads). Because Reader View strips away ads and can sometimes bypass basic paywalls, certain publishers intentionally structure their website code to confuse Safari’s detection algorithm. This forces you to view the standard version of their site.

How to Fix a Grayed-Out Reader View Button

While you cannot change how a website is coded, you can try a few quick steps if the button is unavailable on a page that clearly contains an article:

  • Wait a few seconds: Allow the progress bar in the address bar to finish completely.
  • Refresh the page: Reloading the webpage gives Safari a second chance to detect the text, which is especially helpful on JavaScript-heavy sites.
  • Stop the page from loading early: If a hanging ad script is preventing the page from finishing its load cycle, clicking the "X" in the address bar after the text appears can sometimes force the Reader View button to activate.
  • Click into the specific article: Ensure you are on the direct URL of the story and not reading it from a scrolling feed on a homepage.
