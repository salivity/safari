What Was Safari’s Top Sites 3D Curved Wall View?

Introduced in 2009 with Safari 4, the Top Sites 3D curved wall view was a visually striking feature that displayed a user's most frequently visited websites in a panoramic, concave grid. This article explores the origins, core features, and functionality of the 3D Top Sites view, as well as the design shifts at Apple that led to its flattening and eventual replacement by the customizable Start Page.

The Origins of Top Sites

During the late 2000s, Apple heavily utilized its Core Animation technology to bring three-dimensional, theater-like interfaces to macOS (then OS X). Alongside features like Cover Flow in iTunes and the space-themed Time Machine backup interface, Apple introduced Top Sites in Safari 4.

Before the customizable Start Page existed, opening a new tab or window in Safari presented users with this 3D virtual room. The feature was designed to give users immediate, one-click visual access to their daily web destinations without needing to navigate through text-based bookmark menus.

Key Features and Functionality

The Top Sites view was more than just a static image gallery; it was a dynamic, interactive dashboard.

  • The 3D Panoramic Grid: Websites were displayed as live screenshot thumbnails arranged on a curved wall that mimicked a wrap-around screen, complete with reflections on a glossy virtual floor. Users could choose to display 6, 12, or 24 site previews at once, depending on their screen size and preference.
  • Automatic Population: Safari automatically tracked browsing habits and populated the wall with the user's most visited pages.
  • Manual Customization: By clicking an "Edit" button, the 3D wall would zoom out slightly, allowing users to drag and drop thumbnails to rearrange them. Users could click a thumbtack icon to permanently "pin" a website to a specific slot, or click an "X" to banish a site from ever appearing on the wall.
  • Fresh Content Indicators: Top Sites periodically fetched updates from the displayed websites in the background. If a website had updated its content since the user's last visit, a small blue star appeared in the top-right corner of that website's thumbnail, folded back like a dog-eared page.
  • Zoom Animations: Clicking on a thumbnail triggered a smooth animation where the selected preview physically zoomed forward out of the wall to fill the browser window, seamlessly transitioning into the live webpage.

The Shift to Flat Design and the Start Page

The demise of the 3D curved wall happened in two stages, driven by changing software design trends.

First, in 2014, Apple released OS X Yosemite and Safari 8. Moving away from 3D effects and skeuomorphism (making digital objects look like real-world counterparts), Apple embraced "flat design." Top Sites remained a feature, but the 3D curved wall and glossy floor were removed. In their place was a flat, two-dimensional grid of website thumbnails on a simple translucent background. The blue star indicators for updated content were also removed.

Second, in 2020, Apple released macOS Big Sur and Safari 14, which retired the Top Sites moniker entirely. It was replaced by the modern, customizable Start Page. Instead of exclusively showing a grid of frequently visited sites, the Start Page evolved into a modular hub. It allowed users to set custom background images and toggle various sections on or off, including Favorites, Frequently Visited, Privacy Reports, Siri Suggestions, iCloud Tabs, and the Reading List.
