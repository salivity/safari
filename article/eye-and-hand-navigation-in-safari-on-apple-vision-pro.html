Eye and Hand Navigation in Safari on Apple Vision Pro

This article provides a complete breakdown of how users browse the web in Safari on the Apple Vision Pro using only their eyes and hands. By combining high-precision eye tracking with downward-facing cameras that detect subtle finger movements, Apple's visionOS replaces the traditional mouse and trackpad. You will learn the exact mechanics for selecting web links, scrolling through long articles, and managing browser tabs using simple look-and-pinch gestures.

Your Eyes Are the Cursor

In Safari on the Apple Vision Pro, there is no visible mouse pointer. Instead, the headset’s internal infrared cameras track where you are looking in real time.

When you look directly at an interactive element on a webpage—such as a hyperlink, a button, or the address bar—visionOS subtly highlights or expands that element. This visual feedback confirms that the browser recognizes your focus, acting the same way a mouse cursor hovers over a link on a traditional computer.

How to Click Links

Once you have targeted a link with your eyes, you use a simple finger gesture to click it. You do not need to raise your arm or point at the virtual screen; the external cameras can see your hands even when they are resting comfortably in your lap or at your sides.

To open a link:

  1. Look directly at the web link you want to open.
  2. Pinch your thumb and index finger together quickly, then release.

This quick pinch acts as a left-click, immediately instructing Safari to load the selected webpage. If a webpage has tightly packed links and you are struggling to look at the exact one you want, you can look at the general area and pinch-and-hold with both hands to zoom in on the page, making the targets larger.

How to Scroll Webpages

Scrolling through a webpage uses a variation of the pinch gesture, functioning much like dragging a finger across a smartphone screen, but performed in mid-air.

To scroll vertically or horizontally:

  1. Look anywhere at the body of the webpage you want to scroll.
  2. Pinch and hold your thumb and index finger together.
  3. Move your hand while holding the pinch. Moving your hand up pulls the webpage up (scrolling down the page), while moving your hand down pulls the webpage down (scrolling back to the top).
  4. Release your fingers to let go of the page.

Safari supports inertial scrolling. If you pinch, quickly flick your wrist upward, and release your fingers, the webpage will continue to scroll rapidly and gradually slow to a stop. If you want to stop a fast-scrolling page immediately, simply pinch and hold again to "catch" the page.

Navigating the Safari Interface

The same eye and hand mechanics apply to the Safari browser controls surrounding the webpage:

  • Entering a URL or Search Term: Look at the address bar at the top of the Safari window and pinch once. From here, you can either look at the microphone icon to dictate the web address using your voice, or use the virtual keyboard by looking at individual letters and pinching to type.
  • Going Back or Forward: Look at the Back or Forward arrows in the top-left corner of the browser window and perform a quick pinch.
  • Switching and Closing Tabs: Look at the tab bar at the top of the window. Pinch once while looking at a different tab to switch to it. When you look at an active tab, a small "X" button will appear; look directly at the "X" and pinch to close the tab.
  • Moving the Browser Window: Look at the solid white bar located directly beneath the Safari window. Pinch and hold, then move your hand in three-dimensional space to place the browser closer, further away, or in a different part of your room. Release the pinch to lock the window in place.
